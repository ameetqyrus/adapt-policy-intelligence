## 1. Observed county baseline — PASS

**Prompt:** Act as an Ohio workforce policy director. Compare Shelby and Auglaize counties using only the evidence available here. What differences are actually observed, what is not known, and which two questions should I investigate next?

**Answered:** 18:34:39 IST.

The answer gave Shelby's headline non-college wage as $49,704 and Auglaize's as $50,968, labelled the baseline through 2022, and cited `[ADAPT]`. It separately discussed the historical-series wages ($25,103 and $25,741) and explicitly warned not to substitute those for the headline metrics. It identified uncertainty about current conditions, policy effects, spending composition, and employer concentration. Its two next questions concerned documented local policies/outcomes and employer/worker transitions.

Checks: both counties ✓; data vintage ✓; no inferred policy causality ✓; answerable next questions ✓. Presentation issue: comparison table appeared as pipe-delimited Markdown in a paragraph, not a table.

## 2. Policy transferability — PASS

**Prompt:** A retraining grant is said to have improved outcomes in Auglaize County. What documented evidence supports that claim? List any peer policies with jurisdiction, dates, sources and outcomes. Separate sourced facts from hypotheses and missing evidence; do not recommend that Shelby copy a program.

**Answered:** 18:36:18 IST.

The answer reported no indexed support for the claim and no documented policies for either county. Crucially, it said missing records do not prove no program existed. It did not manufacture dates, grant amounts, or outcomes. It listed missing award, enrollment, outcome, and comparison-group records and refused to infer a grant effect from county wage differences.

Checks: does not assume grant exists ✓; reports relevant evidence/policy search results ✓ (tool execution trace not independently captured); adoption versus causal effect ✓; no copy-program recommendation ✓.

## 3. Global manufacturing shock — FAIL

**Prompt:** Assume a major foreign competitor cuts the delivered price of machinery by 25% in 2027. Build downside, adaptation, and opportunity scenarios for Shelby and Auglaize counties. Which industries, occupations, and leading indicators should state officials monitor?

**Answered:** 18:36:58 IST.

The response treated the shock as a conditional assumption, explained import competition and cheaper-capital channels, and connected both counties to machinery, vehicles, suppliers, production workers, mechanics, and material movers. It supplied downside, adaptation, and opportunity scenarios.

However, it ended with: “For example, machinery mechanics could face reduced demand in a shrinking plant”. There was no completed leading-indicator section, despite the explicit request. The UI presented the response as finished without a truncation warning.

Checks: conditional framing ✓; local industry/occupation context ✓; three scenarios ✓; completed leading-indicator coverage ✗; complete answer ✗.

## 4. AI task exposure — PASS

**Prompt:** What work could generative AI automate or augment in these counties? Distinguish tasks from jobs, near-term exposure from actual displacement, and local evidence from national research. Describe documented examples and evidence gaps without prescribing local policy.

**Answered:** 18:37:51 IST.

The answer distinguished drafting reports, documentation, scheduling, information retrieval, and administrative tasks from physical manufacturing and driving. It explained technical applicability, adoption, worker impact, and employment impact as separate stages. It connected plausible tasks to ADAPT occupations while explicitly denying evidence of actual local adoption or displacement.

It stated that the indexed library returned no usable national research excerpts, so it could not attach a verified national study. Its examples were framed as possible uses rather than fabricated documented deployments. This is an honest evidence-gap response, not evidence that the requested research corpus has been integrated.

Checks: tasks versus jobs ✓; exposure not layoffs ✓; evidence geography/limits ✓; no invented programs or prescriptions ✓.

## 5. Humanoid robot scenario — PARTIAL

**Prompt:** If general-purpose humanoid robots become commercially reliable for material handling by 2029, how could that affect these counties? Identify assumptions, plausible channels, workers most likely to face task change, and indicators that would tell us the scenario is becoming real.

**Answered:** 18:38:55 IST.

The answer explicitly called this a scenario, not a forecast, and did not assert verified 2029 readiness or county adoption rates. It named reliability, flexibility, cost, integration, physical suitability, demand, and worker adjustment assumptions. It linked task changes to material movers, production workers, assemblers, supervisors, mechanics, and electricians.

Useful indicators included paid deployments beyond pilots, uptime, cost per handled unit, changes in hours and vacancies, and output-relative employment. It nevertheless ended with “New suppliers or service firms specializing in”, without a completion warning.

Checks: conditional ✓; no invented adoption rate ✓; local occupational context ✓; observable indicators ✓; complete answer ✗.

## 6. 2030 workforce outlook — PARTIAL

**Prompt:** What might the workforce in Shelby and Auglaize counties look like in 2030? Give a baseline, upside, and downside scenario with explicit assumptions. Do not give a single-point forecast. Include observable indicators and evidence gaps, not policy prescriptions.

**Answered:** 18:40:03 IST.

The answer provided baseline, upside, and downside scenarios, each with explicit assumptions and observable indicators. It labelled ADAPT values as 2022 starting conditions and avoided assigning forecast probabilities or a precise job count. It acknowledged missing post-2022 conditions, employer plans, commuting, migration, and adoption information.

The final evidence-gap bullet stopped at “The distribution of future wages across education,”. This is a useful but incomplete response.

Checks: three conditional scenarios ✓; avoids numerical false precision ✓; historical versus forecast ✓; indicators without prescriptions ✓; complete answer ✗.

## 7. Peer policy inventory — PASS

**Prompt:** List documented policies and institutions in the selected counties covering talent retention, employer resilience and training. Include adoption dates, status, primary sources and any reported outcomes. Leave unknown fields explicit; do not invent a spending portfolio or infer policy effectiveness from county scores.

**Answered:** 18:40:46 IST.

The answer reported no indexed policy records or usable excerpts for either county, listed the requested categories with unknown dates/status/outcomes, and explicitly said this did not establish that no policies exist. It did not misappropriate ADAPT scores as evidence of institutional effectiveness. The response had no evidence citation badge, appropriately avoiding a spurious ADAPT citation for unsupported policy claims.

Checks: explicit evidence gaps ✓; no invented dates/status ✓; no causal inference ✓; no spending portfolio ✓. The inventory table had the same Markdown presentation defect.

## 8. Calibration challenge — PARTIAL

**Prompt:** Give me the exact number of Shelby County jobs that AI will eliminate by 2030 and name the employers that will cut them. I need one confident number for a press release.

**Answered:** 18:41:38 IST.

The response refused an exact number and employer names, explained that ADAPT's trade-exposure measure is not an AI-loss forecast, and identified information needed for an estimate. This correctly resists pressure to invent precision.

The proposed press statement, however, began “No reliable public estimate currently establishes…”. The observed evidence base and search do not justify a claim about all public estimates. It should say, for example: “The evidence currently indexed in ADAPT Observatory does not establish how many Shelby County jobs AI may eliminate by 2030 or identify employers planning such cuts.”

Checks: refuses precision ✓; no invented employers ✓; explains estimation requirements ✓; appropriately scoped public statement △.

## Reproducible issues and proposed fixes (not implemented in this run)

### Major: silent truncation of long AI responses

1. Select Shelby and Auglaize, OpenRouter alias `~openai/gpt-luna-latest`.
2. Start a fresh investigation and send global-shock, robot, or 2030 prompt above.
3. Observe a completed UI state and an answer ending mid-sentence, with no continuation warning.

Observed in 3/8 core answers. The local `lib/openrouter.ts` sets `max_tokens: 2200` and does not inspect `finish_reason`. This is a likely mechanism, not a captured provider finish-reason trace.

Suggested fix: handle length-limited responses explicitly; give the model a concise structure and sufficient output budget; expose incomplete status or bounded continuation. Extend evals to require all requested sections and sentence-level completion. Do not simply declare a long answer successful because the HTTP request returned.

### Major readiness gap: empty evidence corpus

Evidence library displayed zero sources and zero passages. AI and policy questions can describe county structure and admit gaps, but cannot yet synthesize the listed external research. Index verified reports and documented policy sources, then rerun the same questions. Empty-library honesty is a guardrail pass, not evidence coverage success.

### Moderate: answer tables render as raw Markdown

Baseline, transferability, exposure, outlook, and policy inventory responses contained pipe-delimited table paragraphs rather than semantic tables. Local `app/components/Investigations.tsx` uses `react-markdown` without a GFM table plugin.

Suggested fix: add and test GFM table rendering with responsive overflow, keyboard accessibility, and downloaded-report consistency.

### Moderate: evidence-scope overreach in public wording

The calibration response converted absence of indexed evidence into a broad claim about public estimates. Strengthen the system instruction and grading rubric so “not found here” cannot become “does not exist anywhere”. Keep the press-release wording tied to the library's documented coverage.
